# Daily Update — July 27, 2026

*Written for non-technical stakeholders. Plain language, no jargon. Some of this
is still in progress — noted where so.*

## In one line

After last week's outage we built a set of safety guardrails around the database.
Turning the automatic refresh back on this week made those guardrails prove
themselves — they caught every problem cleanly — and in doing so revealed the
*real* cause of the corruption: the server's disk was full.

---

## What we built after the outage

Following the July 24 outage, we hardened the database against corruption so that
a bad refresh can never again fester undetected or destroy our backups. Three
things, in plain terms:

1. **The database now writes more cautiously** (extra safety flush on every save),
   closing one known way a badly-timed interruption can damage the file.
2. **The hourly housekeeping no longer runs while a refresh is in progress** — it
   used to, and that overlap was a prime suspect.
3. **Automatic integrity checks** at three points: when the app starts, when a
   refresh finishes, and before every backup. The key win: corruption is now
   caught **immediately** instead of days later, and a corrupt database can never
   overwrite or age-out our known-good backups.

That third point is what turned this week from a disaster into a series of
five-minute recoveries.

---

## Turning the refresh back on — and what it revealed

With the guardrails in place, we cautiously re-enabled the automatic refresh and
ran it by hand to watch. It failed — but *safely*, and each failure taught us
something:

- **Attempt 1:** the refresh corrupted the database again. But the new integrity
  check caught it the instant it finished, and a fresh backup taken at the start
  let us restore in minutes. This told us our first theory (a power-loss timing
  issue) was **wrong** — the safety flush didn't prevent it.
- **Attempt 2:** the connection to the server dropped mid-refresh (a known risk of
  running it attached to a login session). The refresh stopped safely — its
  "don't delete first" design meant no data was lost.
- **Attempt 3:** the refresh **refused to even start**, reporting the disk was
  full — and correctly aborted rather than write without a backup.

That third failure was the breakthrough.

---

## The real cause: the disk was full

A full refresh writes ~800 MB and needs working room on top of that. The server's
disk had quietly filled to 98%. When a big write runs out of room mid-operation,
it can leave the database file damaged — which matches exactly what we saw. Our
earlier theories (power loss, memory pressure) were red herrings; **this is why my
own test runs always succeeded — my machine has plenty of disk, the server did
not.**

Why did the disk fill? When we restored the fuller database (the one that now
includes the ~125,000 email/activity records), each daily backup jumped from
~250 MB to ~820 MB. Keeping two weeks of those quietly consumed ~11 GB on a 24 GB
disk, and the corrupt copies we'd set aside for investigation added more.

---

## What we're doing about it (in progress)

- **Freeing disk space** — clearing the investigation copies and old Docker
  leftovers (already recovered a few GB), and trimming the oversized backup pile
  down to a few recent, known-good copies.
- **Reducing how many backups we keep** — the two-week retention made sense at
  250 MB per backup, not at 820 MB. Lowering it so the disk can't silently fill
  again.
- **Re-testing the refresh** once there's real headroom. If it completes cleanly
  with disk to spare — as the evidence now strongly suggests — then the automatic
  refresh becomes safe to leave on again, and this whole chapter closes.

The automatic refresh stays **off** until that test passes.

---

## The throughline

Every failure this week was caught immediately and recovered in minutes, with no
data lost and the site never in doubt for long. That is precisely what the
guardrails were built to do. Contrast with the original outage — silent for days,
backups poisoned, cause unknown. The difference is the safety net, and it held.
