# Daily Update — July 28, 2026

*Written for non-technical stakeholders. Plain language, no jargon.*

## In one line

The database incident is resolved: we confirmed the real cause (a full disk),
fixed it, turned the automatic refresh back on safely, and added a one-command
health check. One overnight run remains to watch, then we're back to features.

---

## Confirmed: the cause was a full disk

Yesterday's theory got its proof today. After freeing up disk space, we ran the
**same full refresh that had corrupted the database three times** — and this time
it completed cleanly, end to end, including the heavy email/activity import that
was the exact point of failure before. The database passed its integrity check
afterward. That's as close to certainty as this gets: **when the disk had room,
the problem disappeared.**

Why the disk filled: after we restored the larger database (the one that now
carries ~226,000 email/activity records), each daily backup grew to ~820 MB, and
keeping two weeks of them quietly consumed most of a 24 GB disk. A refresh writes
~800 MB and needs working room on top of that; on a near-full disk it ran out
mid-write and damaged the file.

---

## What we deployed

- **Fewer daily backups kept** — dropped from 14 to 4. Fourteen 820 MB backups was
  ~11 GB; four is manageable and can't silently fill the disk again. (Adjustable
  if we ever want more history on a bigger disk.)
- **A safer backup routine** — it now refuses to let a bad database push out the
  known-good backups, and keeps the safety snapshots separate from the daily ones.
- All of yesterday's hardening (the cautious-write setting, the non-overlapping
  housekeeping, the three integrity checkpoints) is now live on the server.

---

## The automatic refresh is back on

With the disk managed and the safety net proven, we re-enabled the scheduled
refresh:

- **Every two hours during the day** — a quick, light pass that keeps statuses,
  dates and assignments current.
- **A full pass overnight** — the complete refresh, including emails/activities
  and detecting anything deleted in Monday.com.

Every safeguard is in place: an interrupted or failed run leaves the previous data
intact, corruption is caught immediately, and a fresh backup is taken before each
run.

---

## New: a one-command health check

Given how much we've had to hand-check the system this week, there's now a single
command — **`npm run health`** — that reports, green or red: database integrity,
schema, refresh status, record counts, disk space, and how many backups exist. It
can also feed a monitor, alerting only on real problems. Today it reads all-green
on the server.

---

## What's left, and what's next

- **One thing to watch:** tonight is the first *unattended* overnight refresh since
  the incident. A 30-second check tomorrow morning confirms it ran clean and the
  disk stayed healthy. If a problem occurs, the safeguards halt it and the backup
  is the fallback — but a clean full run already happened today, so it should be
  routine.
- **Then: back to features.** Assuming the overnight run is clean, tomorrow we
  return to product work.

---

## Closing note

It was a hard stretch, but the system came out of it markedly more robust than it
went in: a refresh that updates in place instead of wiping and rebuilding, backups
that can't be poisoned or fill the disk, integrity checks at every step, a real
understanding of what went wrong, and a health command to confirm it at a glance.
The safety net didn't just recover us — it turned each failure into a five-minute
fix and, ultimately, into the diagnosis.
